# MAUDE: coded complaint surveillance on FDA device adverse events

Generated by `bench/maude.py report` from `docs/results/maude_runs.json`; do not edit by hand. Every number below is from a live run against `jev-1.13.0`.

**Corpus.** Medical device adverse event reports from openFDA `device/event` (MAUDE), `date_received` 2025-07-01 to 2026-06-30, for three product codes: QBJ (Continuous glucose monitor, the 8th and 22nd of each month, 41,776 reports pulled, 41,748 eligible); FTR (Silicone gel-filled breast implant, 33,176 reports pulled, 30,832 eligible); LWS (Implantable cardioverter defibrillator (non-CRT), 24,619 reports pulled, 24,521 eligible). A report is eligible when its Description of Event or Problem has at least 100 characters and its event type is one of Death, Injury, Malfunction, Other. Per code, dev is 500 reports and test 1,000, drawn by a fixed hash rule stratified by event type with a floor of 50 per event type where that many remain; a disjoint gloss slice of 200 per code is the only source of the criteria's examples. The keys are committed in `bench/maude_ids.json`.

**What is asked.** One fused `jev(description, $questions)` request per report: `problem`, a Choice over the product code's option set (its 40 most frequent filed device-problem terms with at least 5 reports in the pool, plus a catch-all `some other problem`: 40 for QBJ, 35 for FTR, 40 for LWS); `harm`, a Choice over FDA's event types with glosses from 21 CFR 803.3; and `severity`, a Score over five levels from no harm to death.

**How it is measured.** Against the codes the manufacturers filed. *Top-1 in set*: the argmax is one of the report's filed problem terms (a report may carry several), over the reports that have a filed term in the option set; *coverage* is the share that do. *Strict*: exact match on reports with exactly one filed term. *Set mass*: the mean Σp over the filed terms, which tracks top-1 in set when the distribution is calibrated. *Harm accuracy*: argmax equals the filed event type; the *macro average* weighs each event type present equally, since Malfunction dominates QBJ and Injury dominates FTR. *Severity AUROC* ranks Death or Injury above Malfunction by the expected severity level. ECE uses 10 equal-width bins. The chosen round is the selectable round with the best pooled dev top-1 in set, ties to fewer tokens per request: **R3**. The test split ran only with R0 and R3.

- **R0**: baseline: the product code's option set as bare term strings, the description as the state, three questions fused.
- **R1**: R0 with the official FDA definition as structured criteria (`what`) on every option.
- **R2**: R1 plus `not_for` and one example sentence on every option of both Choices: a problem option's `not_for` names its confusable neighbours in the hierarchy, an event type's names its neighbouring type; the examples are written from the gloss slice.
- **R3**: R2 (the best of R0 to R2) with the state as an object: description, brand and generic name, and the manufacturer's additional narrative when present.
- **R4**: R3 (the best round) with both Choices' option order reversed: the order check.
- **R5**: R3 (the best round) over the full vocabulary, every term seen on the corpus, instead of the product code's set; the terms outside the code's set carry their official definition: the candidates-in-code ablation.
- **R6**: R3 (the best round) as three separate requests instead of one fused: the fusion cost check.

R5's option set is every term seen on the corpus, so its coverage and its covered rows differ from the other rounds; on the rows the per-code set covers, R5's top-1 in set is 0.790.

## Reading the numbers

Written by hand from the dev rounds on 2026-09-27 06:27 UTC, before the held-out runs (2026-09-27 06:27 UTC); recorded in the run log and reproduced here unchanged.

**The label is the manufacturer's coding convention, and the rounds taught Jev the convention.** R0 asked for the device problem over bare term strings and got 0.570 top-1 in set on dev. Almost every miss was a report Jev read correctly and coded one level away from where its manufacturer files it: Dexcom files a missed sensor-failure alert as `Protective Measures Problem`, where the narrative reads as `Defective Alarm`; Dexcom files signal loss as `Wireless Communication Problem`, where Jev picked its parent; Allergan files capsular contracture as `Device Appears to Trigger Rejection` and Mentor files the same complication as `Adverse Event Without Identified Device or Use Problem`. The official definitions (R1) moved FTR from 0.448 to 0.677 and left QBJ where it was. Writing the conventions down as `not_for` and one example per option, from the gloss slice only (R2), took QBJ from 0.443 to 0.858 and the pooled number to 0.803; adding the brand name and the manufacturer's own narrative to the state (R3) took FTR to 0.770 and the pooled number to 0.820. Problem ECE fell from 0.160 to 0.051 along the way.

**What R3 still misses is convention the narrative does not carry.** On FTR, 47 of the 114 dev errors are Mentor capsular contracture coded as Allergan codes it, and 20 are `Break` and `Material Rupture` swapped between the two filers who use them for the same event (Establishment Labs files a rupture as `Break`, Mentor as `Material Rupture`). On QBJ, 41 of 70 are low or high readings coded to the parent term: Abbott's US entity files erroneous results under the parent and its UK entity under `Low Readings`, and Dexcom's inaccuracy template often does not say which direction. The next lever is the manufacturer's name in the state, or one option description per filer; both are input changes, and neither was in this plan.

**Harm follows 21 CFR 803 once the rule is stated.** Harm accuracy went from 0.809 to 0.901 (macro 0.860 to 0.921), almost all of it LWS Injury, 0.262 to 0.631: a lead capped, replaced or explanted with no complication is a serious injury under 803.3, and the R2 `not_for` on Malfunction says so. The remaining LWS Injury misses are reports where the filer counted reprogramming or a recommended replacement as the intervention. The fixed rule selects on the problem question, so a harm-only wording round would not be chosen; it is the first harm lever for the next benchmark.

**Order is a per-code lever, not a global one.** Reversing both option lists (R4, a check) moved QBJ from 0.860 to 0.908 and FTR from 0.770 to 0.737. The pooled number moved 0.6 points. Order should be set per product code, and measured per code.

**Candidates in code pay twice.** Offering the full vocabulary of 201 terms instead of each code's set (R5) cost 3.3 points of top-1 in set (0.787 against 0.820), took 12,240 tokens per request instead of 5,123, and ran at 32 reports a second instead of 145 (at concurrency 8, with 1,647 retried 429s). Its distribution was well calibrated (ECE 0.022); the per-code set is simply the better question.

**Fusion is the cost lever.** Three separate requests (R6) gave the same answers within half a point and cost 31% more per report at 2.7 times the wall time.

**What the held-out numbers should look like.** The test split is drawn from the same pools by the same rule and is twice the size, so R3 should land within two points of its dev numbers: top-1 in set near 0.82, harm accuracy near 0.90, problem ECE near 0.05, and R0 near 0.57. Coverage should stay near 0.99. At R3's 5,123 tokens per request and 1.2 reports per request after deduplication, coding the whole 2026 stream to date costs about $450; R0's bare terms cost about a fifth of that for 25 points less. The deferral curve is the operational readout: on dev at confidence 0.9, R3 answers 56% of reports at 0.948 top-1 in set and routes the rest to a person.

## The questions

Problem instructions: `Which device problem does this medical device adverse event report describe? Choose the FDA device problem term the manufacturer would code it with.`

Harm instructions: `Which FDA event type is this medical device adverse event report?`; options and glosses:

- `Death`: The patient died, and the device may have caused or contributed to the death
- `Injury`: A serious injury and no death: the device may have caused or contributed to an injury or illness that was life-threatening, resulted in permanent impairment of a body function or permanent damage to a body structure, or needed medical or surgical intervention to preclude such impairment or damage
- `Malfunction`: The device failed to meet its performance specifications or otherwise perform as intended, and no death or serious injury is reported
- `Other`: Another serious or important medical event that is not a death, a serious injury or a device malfunction

Severity instructions: `How severe was the harm to the patient in this medical device adverse event report?`; levels 0 to 4: `no harm to the patient`; `minor harm that needed no medical treatment`; `harm that needed medical or surgical treatment`; `life-threatening harm or permanent impairment`; `the patient died`.

The judged SQL of a fused round (`questions` holds each product code's question map):

```sql
CREATE OR REPLACE TABLE judged AS
SELECT r.product_code, r.mdr_report_key, jev(r.state_object, q.questions) AS a
FROM reports r JOIN questions q USING (product_code)
```
